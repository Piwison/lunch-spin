Where the centre goes, and what it buys

Drawn to scale on a 390×844 phone with a home indicator, at 17 places — the wheel in your screenshot. The persimmon bar on each is the label band for the wedge under the pointer; the grey ones are its neighbours. The dashed line is the top of the dock.

The catch is in option A vs. "just zoom more". If the type scales with the disc, a bigger wheel fits exactly the same number of characters — band and font grow together and 6.7 stays 6.7. Every option below that gains characters does it by holding the type size while the disc grows, or by wrapping to a second line. Nothing is gained by magnification alone.

That is every name on your wheel in full, with room to spare. The cost is that two lines need the wedge to be wide enough to hold them without the neighbours colliding, which is the constraint the resting wheel already solves by dropping to one line above 8 places — so the zoomed state would need its own rule rather than inheriting the resting tier.
On the bigger hub
The hub grows on its own in A and B — 49px today, 68px at the dock line, 85px at the floor — because it scales with the disc. But growing it faster than the disc works against the names: the label band starts where the hub ends, so every pixel the hub gains is a pixel the band loses. If the hub should read as a deliberate axle rather than just a bigger circle, the cheaper move is to give it a visible edge, not more diameter.
