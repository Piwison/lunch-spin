Why the glass reads flat
backdrop-filter: blur() works — it is blurring a flat gradient, and a blurred flat gradient is the same flat gradient. Glass only becomes glass when there is content behind it.

1 · over flat ground

Filter
36% white over #EFE8DF is ~3% lighter than the ground. Nothing to blur, nothing to refract — it can only be a paler rectangle.

2 · over the wheel

Filter
Same panel, same recipe. The wheel behind it is doing all the work — the blur has structure to soften and colour to pull through.

3 · + the edge

Filter
Plus the rim, the refracting ring and three shadows. This is the target — it now has thickness, not just translucency.
Rim · specular
A gradient ring, not a flat 1px line. Bright at the upper left, dark at 46–62% — a light bevel needs a shadowed side to read on paper.
Lens · refraction
A 14px inset ring re-sampling the backdrop at saturate 1.45 / brightness 1.07. Exactly inset, never a radial fade, so it cannot creep under the content.
Depth · three shadows
1px contact, 8px lift, 20px ambient. One shadow reads as a sticker; three read as an object above a surface.
The rule that follows: glass goes where it overlaps something — the dock over the wheel, the sheet over the wheel, the switcher over the wheel. A glass panel floating on bare ground should be solid paper instead, and secondary buttons ON a glass sheet are outlined, not glass: stacking glass on glass gives you two flat panels.
